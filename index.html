Each phone sends the shop, the amount and the card to this app whenever you tap to pay. The app files it: the card picks Food or Spending and the shop picks the category. Do this once on every phone that pays. The link always opens in Safari, not in the home-screen app, and Safari keeps its own login, so you log in there once as well. It is free and never touches your bank. Amazon, PayPal and other online buys are still added by hand.
Your iPhone software
Settings → General → About shows your iOS version. If a screen looks a little different on your phone, follow the nearest step: Apple moves menus around between updates.

Steps

On your iPhone, one at a time. Blue words are things you tap.

    The link

    In the Open URLs box, build the link in this order. Type each text part (or tap Copy and paste it) and insert each variable where it says so.

    Test it

    Takes about a minute.
    1. Pay for something small with Apple Pay on that phone.
    2. The link opens in Safari (not the home-screen app) and shows what it added, with Undo. Safari keeps its own login, so the first time it asks you to log in: do it on that page and the payment is saved straight after.
    3. A shop the app does not know yet depends on the card. On a Spending card it is marked CHECK in the list. On the Food card it goes in Groceries, so tap Edit and pick Takeaway if it should be. Fix it once and it is remembered.
    Each tap opens the link in Safari for a second. Safari may leave a tab open each time, so close them now and then. If a login screen ever shows when you pay, log in on that page and keep it open: the payment is saved as soon as you are in. Safari can sign you out after about a week of using Safari if you have not tapped anything on this site there. If that happens, log in again on that page. If one tap is ever logged twice, the app skips the repeat.
    Tap to paywith Apple Pay
    Shortcutposts it quietly
    This appfiles it later
    The old link opens Safari, which fails when the phone is locked and wants its own login. Silent capture fixes both: the shortcut drops each Apple Pay tap straight into a private inbox in your database. Nothing opens and nobody logs in. The next time any phone opens this app (or within a minute if it is already open) the app files each one the way the link did: the card picks Food or Spending and the shop picks the category.
    The honest limits
    • Only Apple Pay taps. Plastic cards, online buys, transfers and direct debits are still added by hand.
    • A tap made with no signal is lost. The phone does not send it again.
    • The Wallet trigger can occasionally skip a tap.
    • A payment is counted when someone next opens the app, usually within a day.
    • The date is when the shortcut ran. That is normally the tap, but it can be hours late, so a tap just before payday (the 15th at 10pm) can land in the wrong pay cycle.
    Payments over $1,000, or more than 2 days old, are marked CHECK. A payment that looks like a refund, has no readable amount or is not in dollars is not added: it waits on Home under Needs a look. The old link keeps working until you switch each phone.

    1. Make the codes

    One secret code for each person's phone.
    A lost phone is switched off alone by changing its code. The names are only labels.
    Codes saved in your family's record, which only your logins can read. After you make or change a code, copy the rules again (step 2) and redo the shortcut on that phone (step 4).

    2. Copy the rules

    Done once, in the Firebase console.
    1. Open the Firebase console → Firestore Database → Rules.
    2. First copy the rules that are there now and keep them somewhere safe. If anything goes wrong you paste them back.
    3. Tap Copy rules below. In the console select everything in the Rules box and replace it with what you copied.
    4. Replace the three EMAIL_ lines with your family's login emails: the ones you log in to this app with, in small letters, keeping the quote marks and commas.
    5. Press Publish.
